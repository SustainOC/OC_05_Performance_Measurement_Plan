The situation

Okanagan College has a long measurement record. It has reported its emissions to the Province every year since 2008, and its current state assessment notes that energy and water use is already tracked from the College's own utility data. It reported to STARS in 2011, 2014 and 2023, earning Silver in the first two.

The 2023 report was submitted unscored, as a Reporter, under version 2.2. That designation ends on 19 February 2027. The College's own assessment also notes that STARS changes with each version, so its earlier scores cannot show progress against today's criteria.

The plan now names thirteen headline indicators. Six draw on data the College holds or has measured: energy, renewable supply, water, waste diversion, commuting, and building and fleet emissions. None has a baseline value recorded yet; six are not started, and the score itself is in progress.

STARS 3.0.1 scores a share of applicable points, so the College's result depends on which credits apply as much as on what it earns. Applied research is already out of the denominator. Eight more questions move it between 201 and 224 points, and with it the Silver threshold between 90 and 101.

Where the peers stand

Two of the twelve peer institutions hold a current STARS 3.0 rating, both Gold at about 69 (their figures), and both claimed the full ten bonus points.

Among the colleges, Kwantlen Polytechnic University and the British Columbia Institute of Technology hold current Silver ratings under version 2.2. Outside assurance of a report is rare: one peer claims it.

The performance measurement case

A STARS score is twelve other areas' work, divided by a denominator this area sets. This area's job is to make every one of their numbers count.

The chain runs from a data owner in another area, through a written method, a check by someone who did not collect the figure, and the College's reporting calendar, to a submission AASHE reviews and a result executive leadership uses. A break anywhere along it costs points or trust.

Three things that are true

The score is a ratio, and the denominator is not yet fixed.

A single applicability answer can move the Silver threshold by up to four and a half points. Settling them is the cheapest lever in the plan and has to happen before the November 2026 submission anyway.

This area earns almost nothing directly.

Two bonus points are its own. Everything else in the score is earned by other areas, so the measure of this one is whether their figures arrive measured, inside the window, and documented to each credit's criteria.

A figure without a written method does not hold up at review.

AASHE staff review every scored report for accuracy and adherence to credit criteria before it is published. A written method is what lets the College answer review questions quickly and consistently.

The rest of the picture

One measurement serves two submissions.

Most credits take the most recent data from within the previous three years. A baseline measured in the 2027/28 performance year can carry both the November 2028 and November 2030 reports.

Bonus points weigh more on a small denominator.

Ten bonus points on 201 to 224 applicable points are worth 4.5 to 5.0 percentage points. Both Gold peers took all ten. The plan's bonus credits are spread across several briefs, so one ledger keeps them counted once and stops any being wasted beyond the cap.

The version is about to change.

AASHE says STARS 3.1 is slated for release later in 2026, and its data quality certificate is tied to the version it was earned on. Every brief in the plan maps credits to 3.0.1, so the mapping needs one careful pass when 3.1 lands.

Two rating ladders are in circulation.

The College's current state assessment sets Silver in 2026, Gold in 2028 and Platinum in 2030. The KPI register and every drafted brief use Silver in 2028 and Gold in 2032. The plan cannot run on both, so the choice is the first decision below.

A scored report has entry requirements a Reporter does not.

The manual requires a paid STARS subscription and a letter from a senior executive for a rated report. An unscored report needs neither. Whether the November 2026 report is rated turns on those two items.

How this was worked out

Score: points earned plus bonus points (up to 10), divided by applicable points, as a percentage. Credits that do not apply leave the denominator rather than scoring zero. Thresholds: Platinum 85, Gold 65, Silver 45, Bronze 25. A rating is valid for three years and may be renewed once a year.

Applicable points: 250 in the manual's table of credits. Research (23) is out by the College's settled decision. Investment holdings drop from 6 to 3 points for any pool under one billion US dollars. Graduate programs (8) apply only with 25 or more master's or doctoral qualifications. The upper bound, 224, keeps every other gated credit in. The lower bound, 201, also removes every credit whose gate is still to be determined, and keeps those the College is expected to meet: construction and dining.

Points needed: 45 per cent of 201 is 90.5 and of 224 is 100.8; 65 per cent is 130.7 and 145.6. These are thresholds, not forecasts.

The target, stated three times

One outcome in this area's own unit, one lever AASHE scores, and a third card for the part of the score this area controls alone. The third card is a deliberate deviation from the two-card pattern.

Outcome target · this area's own unit

By the end of FY2027/28 every headline indicator in the plan carries a measured baseline, a written method and a named data source. From then on each is refreshed, checked and reviewed with executive leadership every year, and every STARS submission and carbon filing goes in on schedule.

Baseline: 0 of 13 headline indicators with a baseline recorded; 6 of 13 with a data source named; 18 consecutive carbon filings; last STARS report December 2023.

Why this one: it measures the desired outcome's own words, transparent and reliable measurement used in decisions. It is self-assessed, so it is paired with a score AASHE sets.

Lever target · a STARS-scored unit

The College's STARS score reaches 45 per cent of applicable points or more (Silver) at the November 2028 submission, holds it in 2030, and reaches 65 or more (Gold) at November 2032.

Baseline: not measured under 3.0.1. Last scored 55.85 in 2014 under version 1.1, not comparable.

Why this one: it is the plan's headline indicator for this area, scored by AASHE, and it is the ladder every drafted brief already plans against. The Gold peers sit at 68.83 and 69.12 (their figures).

Third target · recorded as a deliberate deviation

Reporting assurance and data quality bonus credits at 1.5 of 2 points by November 2028 and 2 of 2 from November 2030.

Baseline: 0 claimed. The 2023 report was unscored and claimed neither.

Why this one: the score is moved by twelve other areas, so a third card holds the part this area controls alone. One peer claims full outside assurance; this target matches it by 2030.

How the options were chosen

The axis tested and adopted is the depth of independent checking, taken from the desired outcome's word "reliable". Four others were tested and rejected. Pace up the rating ladder was rejected because the score is earned by twelve other areas, so tiers built on it would restate their targets; it is carried as the first decision instead. Outside money was rejected because spending here is mostly staff time. Dependence on outside parties was rejected because AASHE review applies in every option. Reporting frequency separates only two options and sits inside option 3.

Every cost line reads "not yet costed" where no College figure exists. No figure in the table is estimated.

The decisions we need

Five decisions. The second and fourth have to be settled before the November 2026 submission. None commits capital.

Which rating ladder does the plan commit to?

Two are in circulation: Silver in 2026, Gold in 2028 and Platinum in 2030 in the College's current state assessment, and Silver in 2028 and Gold in 2032 in the KPI register and every drafted brief. Recommendation: commit to Silver 2028 and Gold 2032, submit in November 2026 for whatever rating the evidence supports, and revisit the pace once the first 3.0.1 result is known.

Should the November 2026 report be submitted for a rating?

A rated report needs a paid subscription and a senior executive's letter; an unscored one renews the Reporter designation and leaves the score unmeasured until 2028. Recommendation: submit for a rating, so the headline indicator has a baseline two years sooner.

Should every submission be independently reviewed before it leaves the College?

Independent review protects points at AASHE review and earns half a bonus point; peer assurance from 2030 earns the other half. Recommendation: adopt option 2, with reviewers drawn from functions outside each credit's data collection.

Who answers the applicability questions, and by when?

Eight of nine are open, and their answers sit with financial planning, institutional records, purchasing, building operations and campus planning and capital. Recommendation: answer all before the November 2026 submission, coordinated by the Sustainability Office.

Should this area keep the plan's bonus point ledger?

Bonus credits are counted in several briefs and capped at ten for the College as a whole. Recommendation: each point stays counted in its own brief, and this area tracks the total against the cap.
